1. **Broken float32 tie-breaking and `cdist` cancellation in E4b — [e4b_neighbors.py](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/onestep-discrete/sweeps/e4b_neighbors.py#L18-L21), [e4b_neighbors.py](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/onestep-discrete/sweeps/e4b_neighbors.py#L34), [REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L46), [REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L268), [REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L278)**
   - **Claim:** Euclidean nearest neighbors with "random tie-breaking"; 22-cell Spearman correlation is `0.88`.
   - **What is wrong:** `d = torch.cdist(z, z) + 1e-6 * torch.rand(...)` runs in `float32`. At $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142135$, `float32` ULP is $2^{-23} \approx 1.19 \times 10^{-7}$, so `1e-6 * U[0, 1]` spans only ~8.4 ULPs. Roughly 12% of tied entries round back to exact `1.4142135382`, and `argmin(1)` deterministically picks the lowest column index. In addition, default `float32` `torch.cdist` uses matrix-multiply expansion ($\|a\|^2 + \|b\|^2 - 2 a\cdot b$), introducing up to `1.38e-3` cancellation error on `quant`/`shuf`.
   - **Evidence (recomputed against `float64` exact distances + `1e-12` jitter):**
     - On all `onehot` cells (all 4,095 non-self distances equal $\sqrt{2}$), all 4,096 queries collapse onto **95–101 unique target indices** (indices `0..140`, median `11`, vs ~2,563 expected under uniform tie-breaking). Seed spread inflates to `0.020–0.025`, shifting `markov onehot/ot` from exact `0.3024` to stored `0.3113` (`+0.0089`) and `mix16 onehot/random` from `0.2691` to `0.2747` (`+0.0056`).
     - Because `markov onehot/ot` has the highest chance-cluster accuracy (`0.3742`) and received that `+0.0089` upward bias, 22-cell Spearman rises from **`0.853`** (exact `float64`, same seeds; `0.869 ± 0.008` over 1,000 unbiased MC tie-breaks) to stored **`0.876`** (`0.88`). Pearson is essentially unchanged (`0.9344` vs `0.9347`).
     - On `code` (18-way Hamming-1 ties), ties collapse to ~9 `float32` buckets (`same_nn = 0.055`, mean `nu_tok` shifts `≤ 0.0021`).
     - On `quant/kr` and `shuf/kr`, `0.43%` and `0.37%` of points pick strictly non-nearest neighbors (`nu_tok` shift `≤ 0.0002`).

3. **Unmeasured MC evaluation bias in E7c attributed solely to boundary-fitting error — [e7c_ode_teacher.py](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/onestep-discrete/sweeps/e7c_ode_teacher.py#L46-L52), [REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L166-L167)**

### What Checks Out

- **Proposition A ([REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L105-L118)):** Fully rigorous. CE-optimality gives $q(x_i \mid z) = \pi(x_i \mid z)$; $\mathrm{supp}(\pi_X) \subseteq \mathrm{supp}(p_G)$ holds automatically; joint-vs-marginal KL monotonicity yields $\mathrm{KL}(\pi_X \| p_G) \le \mathbb{E}_z TC(X \mid Z=z)$; the identity $\mathbb{E}_z TC(X \mid Z=z) = TC(X) - [\sum_i I(Z; X_i) - I(Z; X)]$ is exact (in nats, matching code); equality at $I(Z; X) = 0$ and conditional independence, plus failure of the converse, all check out.
- **Proposition B ([REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L180-L187)):** Mathematically exact (for $K \ge |\mathrm{supp}(p)|$). Marginal gains $\Delta_c(n) = p_c \log(1 + 1/n)$ are strictly decreasing in $n \ge 1$, so greedy allocation from $n_c = 1$ is globally optimal (KKT optimality verified directly for all $K \in \{16, 64, 256, 1024, 4096, 65536\}$). Upper-bound (non-floor) remark for soft outputs is correct and verified by E8 `categorical K=16` seeds (`0.3875, 0.3786 < 0.4137`).
- **Proposition C ([REPORT.md](file:///mnt/d/CS/GenerativeModels/One-Step-Should-be-Continuous/REPORT.md#L189-L196)):** Sound modulo the minor notation caveats above. Reynolds transport / coarea formula holds for smooth boundaries; atomic mass is piecewise constant (zero derivative a.e.); and the Hamming-distance-$\ge 2$ loss barrier for soft product outputs is rigorously true (any perturbation moving an atom off codeword $c_1$ toward $c_2$ at distance $d_H \ge 2$ suffers first-order $O(\epsilon)$ loss on $c_1$ against $O(\epsilon^{d_H})$ gain on $c_2$, making every full-support deterministic allocation a strict local minimum even prior to softmax saturation).
