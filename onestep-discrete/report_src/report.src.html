Why one-step generators get their randomness from continuous noise

Bits are cheap and representation is not the problem. What discrete noise lacks is what one-step training relies on: a deterministic coupling to learn from, mass that can move, and geometry that makes the coupling learnable.

Toy world: \(V = 4\) tokens, \(L = 6\) positions, \(4{,}096\) states. Every distribution is enumerated, so KL, TV and total correlation are exact (no FID, no perplexity).

The question

A one-step generator draws a source sample \(Z\), runs one forward pass, and samples every position independently:

\[ p_\theta(x) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{Z}\prod_{i=1}^{L} p_\theta(x_i \mid Z). \]

Every correlation between positions must therefore be carried by \(Z\). Every one-step generator that works well, including the discrete ones, gets \(Z\) from continuous noise. What exactly is missing from a discrete source of randomness, and how much of it is a hard information-theoretic limit versus a soft limit of geometry and optimization?

The answer

The hard limit is real but cheap. A uniform source needs at least \(\operatorname{rank}(p)\) atoms. Above that, discrete noise can represent the data almost exactly, and well-separated atoms even fit an equal-weight mixture better than a Gaussian. The soft limits are what separate discrete from continuous noise:

  1. ②A deterministic coupling. A one-step student distilled from a coupling \(\pi(z,x)\) errs by at most \(\mathbb{E}_z\,\mathrm{TC}(X\mid Z=z)\), which is zero when the coupling is deterministic. Marginalizing noise → data dynamics gives a deterministic ODE map in continuous space and a branching chain in discrete space.
  2. ①Mass that can move. With \(K\) atoms, mode masses are multiples of \(1/K\) and piecewise constant in \(\theta\). With a density, mass flows across moving boundaries.
  3. ③Aligned geometry. A coupling generalizes to unseen \(z\) only if nearby sources map to similar outputs. Discreteness costs nothing here; disorder costs everything.

Continuous noise provides all three for free. Discrete noise supplies bits, but none of these by default.

Setup

  • Data. mix16: uniform mixture of 16 random product distributions. markov: first-order chain. copy: \(x_1 = \dots = x_6\). code<r>: \(r\) codewords of the distance-2 parity code (rank exactly \(r\)). zipf16: 16 codewords with Zipf weights.
  • Generator. MLP \(z \to 256 \to 256 \to 6\times 4\) logits, SiLU, Adam with cosine decay.
  • Objectives. Either the marginal likelihood \(-\sum_x w(x)\log \frac1N\sum_{n}\prod_i p_\theta(x_i\mid z_n)\), identical for every source within an experiment, or supervised regression (cross-entropy) on \((z, x)\) pairs from a coupling.
  • Metrics. Exact for finite sources with \(K \le 2^{16}\). Continuous sources use a \(2^{16}\)-sample estimate of \(p_\theta\) (bias \(\le 3.4\%\), median \(0.2\%\)). Three seeds unless stated.

Preliminaries: bits are cheap

A mixture of \(k\) product distributions fits \(p\) exactly iff \(k \ge \operatorname{rank}_+(p)\); EM gives \(\mathrm{KL} = \mathrm{TC}\) at \(k = 1\) and \(\le 3\cdot 10^{-6}\) for \(k > r\) (E1). A \(k\)-step masked-diffusion sampler manufactures its own shared randomness: at \(k = 1\) the all-mask source has \(H(Z) = 0\) and \(\mathrm{KL} = \mathrm{TC}\) exactly, and each further step adds committed tokens (E2). Once \(H(Z)\) passes \(\log_2 \operatorname{rank}\), representation stops being the bottleneck. On mix16 every source fails alike below 4 bits, and above it the best fits come from well-separated atoms (one-hot, categorical, Rademacher: 0.0004–0.014 nats), not from a Gaussian (0.055). The plan's conjecture that structureless discrete noise is worse is falsified as stated (E3).

②The coupling: a student inherits its conditional dependence

At scale, one-step generators are not trained by likelihood (it needs \(\sim 2^{I(X;Z)}\) source samples per step, E6) but by regressing onto a coupling \((z, x)\) from a teacher or an encoder. What does a coupling give a one-step student?

Proposition A. Let \(\pi(z,x)\) be any coupling with \(X\)-marginal \(\pi_X\), and let the student be regression-optimal, \(q(x_i\mid z) = \pi(x_i\mid z)\), so that \(p_G(x) = \sum_z \pi(z)\prod_i \pi(x_i\mid z)\). Then \[ \operatorname{KL}(\pi_X \,\|\, p_G) \;\le\; \mathbb{E}_z\, \mathrm{TC}(X\mid Z=z) \;=\; \mathrm{TC}(X) - \Big[\sum_{i=1}^{L} I(Z;X_i) - I(Z;X)\Big]. \]

Proof. The KL between marginals is at most the KL between the joints \(\pi(z,x)\) and \(\pi(z)\prod_i\pi(x_i\mid z)\), which equals \(\mathbb{E}_z \operatorname{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid z)\,\|\,\prod_i \pi(x_i\mid z)\big) = \mathbb{E}_z\,\mathrm{TC}(X\mid Z=z)\). Expanding, \(\sum_i H(X_i\mid Z) - H(X\mid Z) = \sum_i H(X_i) - \sum_i I(Z;X_i) - H(X) + I(Z;X)\). \(\blacksquare\)

The bound vanishes iff the coupling is conditionally independent across positions given \(z\). A deterministic coupling \(x = T(z)\) is the simplest such case, and then the student is exact. (The converse can fail.) At \(I(Z;X) = 0\) the bound equals \(\mathrm{TC}(X)\) and is attained. What matters is how much each position learns from \(z\), not the entropy \(H(Z)\) of the noise.

Proposition B′ (why marginalized teachers differ). A teacher is built by averaging conditional dynamics over an independent coupling of noise and data. In \(\mathbb{R}^n\) the average is a velocity field whose ODE has unique solutions, so the endpoint map is deterministic and the bound is 0. In a finite space the average is a jump-rate matrix: one state can jump to several targets, so the endpoint is random given the start.

Continuous diffusion's SDE sampler is as uninformative as a reversed discrete chain (\(I(Z_T;X_0)\to 0\)), but its probability-flow ODE has the same marginals and a deterministic coupling. That is what consistency models, flow maps, MeanFlow, FMLM and Duo distill.

We measure this for three kinds of teachers in one harness:

  • E7, forward joints \(\pi(z,x) = p(x)\prod_i k_\alpha(z_i\mid x_i)\) with uniform or masking kernels, computed exactly. At \(\alpha = 0\) this is the reversed noising chain started from its prior.
  • E7b, exact Discrete Flow Matching teacher: the marginal CTMC of the mixture path from \(\mathrm{Uniform}(V^L)\), with the joint of \((X_0, X_1)\) integrated over all \(4096\times 4096\) pairs (Euler error \(\le 0.0073\) nats).
  • E7c, continuous teacher: a flow-matching model on one-hot data, Euler ODE from \(z\sim\mathcal N(0, I_{24})\), then argmax. It is deterministic, so \(I(Z;X) = H(T(Z))\) and the bound is 0. A student is trained on \(2^{22}\) pairs and evaluated against the teacher's own law \(p_T\).

  • The bound holds on all 63 discrete points, is attained at \(I = 0\) and \(\alpha = 1\), and is loose in between (KL/bound down to 0.04–0.14).
  • It explains the DFM offset. At equal \(I(Z;X)\), DFM students sit 8–10% above the forward-joint curves; at equal bound, 0.6–3.4% below. A DFM coupling gives each position less information for the same joint information (\(\sum_i I(Z;X_i) = 1.68\) vs. \(\approx 1.80\) bits on mix16).
  • It does not explain copy's kernel gap. On copy every \(X_i\) determines \(X\), so \(\sum_i I(Z;X_i) = L\cdot I(Z;X)\) for every coupling and the bound depends on \(I\) alone. The masking kernel's 1.2–1.7× advantage lies in the slack of the first inequality; it remains unexplained.
  • Continuous closes the gap. The ODE teacher's student reaches 0.020–0.023 nats, 50–61× below the exact DFM student, with held-out token accuracy 0.97–0.98. What remains is boundary fitting, not information.

Deterministic discrete couplings exist (inverse-CDF maps, OT assignments, CTMCs whose state remembers the start, iterative rectification as in ReDi), but they must be constructed rather than inherited, and they are only learnable with aligned geometry (③).

①Mass: atoms cannot move it, a density can

On zipf16 the exact KL splits into allocation error (how mass is shared among the 16 codewords) and leakage (mass outside them).

Proposition B (the \(1/K\) lattice). If a generator maps \(K \ge |\operatorname{supp} p|\) equal-weight atoms deterministically to states, then \(p_G(x) = n_x/K\) with integer \(n_x\), and \[ \min \operatorname{KL}(p\,\|\,p_G) \;=\; \min_{\substack{n_c \ge 1\\ \sum_c n_c = K}}\; \sum_c p_c \log\frac{p_c K}{n_c}, \] the KL of \(p\) rounded to the \(1/K\) grid. The objective is separable and convex in the integers, so greedy allocation is exact. For soft outputs it is an upper bound on the best KL, not a floor.
Proposition C (flux). For a source with density \(\rho\) and a region \(R_c(\theta)\) whose boundary moves smoothly with \(\theta\), \[ \partial_\theta\, P\big(Z \in R_c(\theta)\big) \;=\; \int_{\partial R_c} \rho(z)\, v_n(z)\, \mathrm{d}S, \] where \(v_n\) is the boundary's outward normal velocity: mass flows wherever there is density on the boundary. For \(K\) atoms, \(P(Z\in R_c(\theta)) = \tfrac1K\,\#\{k : z_k \in R_c(\theta)\}\) is piecewise constant in \(\theta\), with zero derivative almost everywhere. With softmax outputs it is differentiable, but moving an atom between codewords at Hamming distance \(\ge 2\) crosses product distributions that leak onto non-codewords, and saturated atoms have vanishing gradients.

E8 trains finite sources with the exact enumerated objective, the most favorable case. They settle their allocation in the first 1k steps and then move it by at most ±6%; for \(K \ge 64\) their total KL stays 12× to more than \(10^4\)× above the Proposition B optimum. A Gaussian drives its allocation error to \(\approx 2\cdot 10^{-4}\) (half of it evaluation noise) and achieves the best total KL (0.018).

E8 differs from the Gaussian in three ways at once: atoms, geometry, and exact versus Monte Carlo gradients. E8b removes the last two. ③Geometry: a coupling is learnable when it is smooth in the source

E4 fits the generator by regression on 3,276 fixed \((z, x)\) pairs and tests on 820 held-out \(z\). code: the 4,096 symbols as per-position tokens (the representation of uniform-diffusion noise). KR: the Knothe–Rosenblatt map, an autoregressive inverse-CDF sampler driven by \(u\in[0,1]^6\), i.e. a multi-step discrete sampler whose randomness has been moved into \(Z\). The best \(z\)-independent predictor scores 0.38 / 0.35.

  • Geometry is not. The same pairs through one-hot symbols, or the same source law with shuffled levels, fall to the \(z\)-independent baseline. At scale the generator never sees most \(z\), so this is decisive.

E4b asks what "aligned" means. Let the neighbor agreement \(\nu\) be the fraction of positions on which \(T(z)\) and \(T(z')\) agree, where \(z'\) is the nearest other source point in the geometry the generator sees: a discrete Lipschitz proxy for the coupling, computed without training.

  • \(\nu\) at chance ⇒ no generalization. Every geometry-free cell has \(\nu = 0.27\)–\(0.33\), near the agreement of independent draws, and accuracy at the baseline. Every geometric cell has \(\nu \ge 0.46\) and accuracy \(\ge 0.69\).
  • Within a geometry, \(\nu\) orders learnability exactly (Gaussian VAE < OT < ODE in both). Across all 22 cells, Spearman 0.85.
  • Not one curve. The 24-D Gaussian ODE cell (\(\nu = 0.57\), accuracy 0.91) beats the 6-D KR cells (\(\nu = 0.74\), 0.86): nearest neighbors among 4,096 points in \(\mathbb{R}^{24}\) are far apart. Learnability needs \(\nu\) above chance and grows with it; \(\nu\) alone does not determine it.

What this means for one-step discrete generators

  • Bits are not the issue. Uniform-token noise has \(L\log_2 V\) bits, far more than needed. Mask-type noise has 0 bits, which is why one-step masked-diffusion sampling is a product (\(\mathrm{KL} = \mathrm{TC}\)).
  • Learn from a deterministic transport, with noise where mass can move. Continuous noise with an ODE-derived coupling provides both, which is why FMLM, Categorical Flow Maps, Duo and Coupling Models work.
  • A marginalized discrete teacher is a weak source of couplings. Its student is limited by \(\mathbb{E}_z\,\mathrm{TC}(X\mid Z=z)\): the reversed chain keeps \(I(Z;X) = 0\), an exact DFM teacher about 1.3 of 8.6 bits. Build the coupling instead (OT, inverse-CDF, rectification).
  • If the noise must be discrete, make it dense and ordered, aligned with the coupling, and use many atoms (\(\gg 10^3\) here) so that mass can still move.

Limitations

  • Toy scale: \(L = 6\), \(V = 4\), one MLP architecture, budgets of 4k–16k steps. Effects of attention and long sequences are not tested; the real-checkpoint probe (FMLM on LM1B) was not run.
  • The flow-matching teacher is imperfect on mix16 (TV to \(p\) of 0.29, a velocity-fit error), so E7c reports the student's KL to the teacher's own law; against \(p\) it is 0.33, still 4× better than DFM.
  • E4 trains on 3,276 pairs, so held-out accuracy, not deployed KL, is the learnability metric. \(\nu\) is a finite-sample proxy whose scale depends on the source dimension.
  • E8 and E8b use one data family and E8b one source dimension; the mechanism is general, the magnitudes and the \(K\sim 10^3\) crossover are not.

Appendix

A1. Rank threshold (E1)

Population EM, best of 10 restarts. \(\mathrm{KL} = \mathrm{TC}\) at \(k = 1\) to machine precision; \(\le 3\cdot 10^{-6}\) for every \(k > r\). At \(k = r\), single restarts often stall (mix16: 0.03–0.11) while \(k = 1.5r\) always reaches 0: assigning mass is already the hard part (①).

A3. Source sweep details (E3, E3b)
  • Below 4 bits every source fails alike (1.74–1.75 / 1.22–1.24 / 0.67–0.68 nats at 1 / 2 / 3 bits).
  • Above it: one-hot 0.0004–0.010, categorical \(K = 256\) 0.003, Rademacher 0.008–0.014; quantized \(m = 16\) 0.05–0.06, Gaussian 0.055, uniform 0.045. Dense low-dimensional grids and huge \(K\) do worse.
  • The plateau of smooth sources is slow optimization: with 16k steps and \(N = 1024\), Gaussian reaches 0.012 versus \(5\cdot 10^{-5}\) for Rademacher \(d = 4\) (E3b, one seed).
  • Generalization (\(n = 500\) samples): what helps is density, many levels or atoms relative to \(n\). Gaussian and uniform reach about 0.42; Rademacher generalizes as badly as one-hot (0.81–0.96 vs 0.77–0.99).
A4. Shared decision boundaries (E5)

Perturbing \(z\) slightly, adjacent positions on markov co-flip 6.7–8.3× more than chance with a Gaussian source (Spearman 0.65–0.85 against mutual information), and 2.1× with a Rademacher hypercube. Correlation is carried by shared boundaries in any source with neighborhood structure.

A5. The likelihood needs \(N \gtrsim 2^{I}\) source samples per step (E6)

On code<r>, \(N \le r\) fails for every source; a Gaussian needs \(N \gg r\) (code256 is still at 0.68 with \(N = 4096\)). The exact enumerated objective is worse than resampling (0.13–0.20) with flat trajectories, which previews ①. At scale \(I(X;Z)\) is hundreds of bits, so practice regresses onto couplings (②, ③).
